The first "AI superfactory"

On 12 Nov 2025 Microsoft announced it had linked two purpose-built AI data centers — Fairwater 1 (Mount Pleasant, Wisconsin) and Fairwater 2 (Atlanta, Georgia), ~700 miles and five states apart — into a single distributed training system over a dedicated optical backbone it calls the AI-WAN. This is the reference architecture for the "scale-across" era: training jobs that no longer fit in one building, one campus, or even one power grid.

The thesis in one line
Scale-across turns the wide-area network into part of the computer. Once a training job spans data centers 700 miles apart, the interconnect between them stops being "connectivity" and becomes a first-class tier of the supercomputer — a dedicated, low-jitter, massive-capacity optical backbone. For an optical SE, this is the continental-scale, terrestrial cousin of the DCI / dark-fiber demand the ASEAN reports already track: the AI build-out is now buying long-haul coherent transport, not just intra-DC fabric.

Why Microsoft is doing this (the real driver)

  • One building can't hold a frontier training run anymore. GPU counts, power and cooling for a single job now exceed what any one campus can deliver — so the job is spread across sites and stitched back together.
  • Power and grid arbitrage. Spreading load across states and time zones lets Microsoft place work where power, cooling and (increasingly) renewable/firmed energy are available, instead of forcing a single multi-GW draw onto one grid interconnect.
  • Fungibility. With a fast backbone, training, fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, synthetic-data generation, evaluation and inference can be shifted to wherever capacity is free — the fleet behaves as one pool.
  • Speed. Microsoft's framing: training jobs that took months now run in weeks because the sites work as one system.

What matters here

The physical story is impressive but familiar (dense GPUs + liquid cooling + huge power). The genuinely new thing is that Microsoft stopped treating these as independent regions and started treating geographically separate campuses as one machine. That decision is what created the AI-WAN — and the optical demand behind it.

Scale Up → Scale Out → Scale Across

The core mental model. Microsoft (and NVIDIA) describe AI infrastructure as three concentric network tiers, each an order of magnitude larger in distance and looser in latency than the one inside it. Your question — DC connectivity — lives in tiers 2 and 3.

Tier 1 · inside the rack
SCALE-UP
NVLink — 72 GPUs act as one big GPU
Tier 2 · inside the site
SCALE-OUT
800G Ethernet fabric — 100,000s of GPUs, one campus
Tier 3 · between sites
SCALE-ACROSS
AI-WAN — dedicated optical backbone, 100s of miles

Scale-Up

Within one rack, NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 ties 72 Blackwell GPUs together with NVLink/NVSwitch into a single shared-memory domain.

  • 1.8 TB/s per-GPU NVLink bandwidth
  • ~14 TB pooled fast memory per rack
  • up to ~865,000 tokens/s per NVL72
  • ~140 kW per rack; ~1,360 kW per row

Copper/NVLink domain — no optics here. This is "one giant GPU."

Scale-Out

Across racks within a campus, a two-tier, non-blocking fat-tree connects hundreds of thousands of GPUs at 800 Gbps GPU-to-GPU.

  • Ethernet-led (SONiC / open switching) + InfiniBand; avoids single-vendor lock-in
  • Packet trimming, packet spray, high-frequency telemetry, AI-tuned congestion control
  • Two-story building used as a latency tool — shorter, more uniform fiber runs

Intra-DC optics: short-reach pluggables (DR/FR/2xFR 800G) over campus fiber.

Scale-Across

Between campuses, the AI-WAN carries the traffic that no longer fits in one site — over hundreds of miles of dedicated fiber.

  • Dedicated lanes isolated from general cloud traffic (a "carpool lane" for AI)
  • Routes engineered as straight as feasible; minimise optical↔electrical conversions
  • Higher latency, massive capacity — for cross-site training, checkpoint replication, RL, eval, inference

This is the optical / DWDM / DCI tier — the subject of this brief.

AI-WAN — the scale-across layer up close

The AI Wide-Area Network is the part of this project that is genuinely a networking story. What Microsoft has disclosed, and what it implies for the optical layer.

What Microsoft has stated

  • A dedicated, private optical backbone — not the public internet, not shared with general Azure traffic. Purpose-built for AI synchronization.
  • Scale of fiber: ~120,000 fiber-miles deployed/lit across the US in the past year, +25% footprint. A mix of newly built and repurposed routes.
  • "Congestion-free, near the speed of light": routes engineered to minimise hops, jitter and burst congestion; kept as physically straight as feasible to cut "route inflation."
  • Minimise O-E-O conversions: the protocol stack and path are tuned to avoid unnecessary optical↔electrical hops end-to-end.
  • MRC (Multi-Path Reliable Connected): a transport protocol co-developed by Microsoft, NVIDIA and OpenAI — packet trimming/spray, agile multi-path load-balancing, fast retransmit, AI-tuned congestion control. Not yet an open/RFC standard
  • Workload split (phase-aware placement): latency-sensitive pre-training anchors in one site; reinforcement learning, synthetic-data generation and evaluation spill across campuses; checkpoints replicate over the WAN.
Number to handle carefully: the "~120,000 fiber-miles" figure is quoted by different outlets as either new fiber in the past year or total dedicated fiber, and Microsoft's own wording ("expanding … by more than 25%") reads as an annual addition. Treat it as "≈120,000 fiber-miles added in the last year, ~+25% footprint" and verify against the primary post before quoting a customer.

The physics Microsoft doesn't spell out (but the numbers force)

Microsoft has not named an optical vendor, a line rate, a baud rate, or whether the backbone is dark fiber vs leased spectrum. But the requirements it does state constrain the physical layer hard:

  • Latency is fiber-length-bound. Light in fiber travels ~5 µs/km. ~700 miles ≈ ~11–12 ms round-trip just in glass, before switching/regen. That's why pre-training stays in-site and only latency-tolerant phases cross the WAN — you cannot hide 11 ms inside a tight gradient-sync loop.
  • "Minimise O-E-O" = maximise optical reach per span. That is the textbook argument for high-baud coherent transport (fewer regen sites, lower cost and power per bit) — 400G/800G ZR/ZR+ pluggables for shorter metro hops, and long-haul coherent (≈130–160 GBaud, 800G–1.2T/wavelength class) for the 700-mile leg.
  • "Dedicated lanes" over "repurposed fiber" = an open line system the operator provisions and prioritises itself — consistent with the same open, disaggregated philosophy Microsoft applies to SONiC Ethernet.
Interpretation, not disclosure: everything in this section about coherent baud, ZR+/long-haul mix and open line systems is inference from the stated requirements, not something Microsoft published. What the other clouds are doing

Microsoft did not invent scale-across — every frontier lab hit the same wall (one campus can't power or cool a frontier training run) and each answered it differently. The split that matters for optics: distributed across sites (Microsoft, Google) vs one giant campus (Meta, Oracle/OpenAI, xAI). Both generate optical demand — but different shapes of it.

The read for a DWDM SE — two architectures, two optical shapes

  • Distributed camp (Microsoft AI-WAN, Google cross-region) → the richest long-haul coherent DWDM / DCI demand. This is the camp that validates the exact story you sell.
  • Campus camp (Meta, Oracle/OpenAI Stargate, xAI) → less long-haul, but huge intra-campus + metro DCI and very high pluggable (DR/FR, 400/800ZR+) volume. Still optical money, just shorter reach.
  • Google is the incumbent to benchmark — it owns its backbone and subsea, so it buys less merchant optics; treat it as the proof-of-concept, not a customer.
  • AWS & Meta are Ethernet-fabric-led at campus scale — the near-term optics there is components and metro DCI, not turnkey long-haul.
  • Bottom line: whichever camp your regional accounts copy, the optical layer grows — distributed pulls coherent long-haul, campus pulls metro + pluggables. Know which model an account is chasing before you pitch.

Optics / DCI Read

What the superfactory means for the optical market and for Nokia ON specifically. This whole tab is analyst interpretation built on the disclosed architecture — the SE layer, not a Microsoft statement.

The one connection to your world
"Scale-across" is a new, large, self-generated demand class for long-haul coherent DWDM. Hyperscalers used to buy DCI mostly to connect a metro of availability zones. The superfactory model makes them buy continental long-haul coherent transport to train a single model — the same product family Nokia sells (coherent transponders + optical line systems + ZR/ZR+ pluggables), now pulled by AI training rather than by internet backbone growth.

The Nokia ON angle — honest read

Tailwind: Nokia's optical portfolio post-Infinera (1830 GX/PSS line systems, PSE-6s / high-baud coherent, ICE6, and 400/800ZR+ pluggables) is aimed squarely at exactly Tier 3 — metro DCI and long-haul coherent. Scale-across is a structural new driver for that portfolio.
Reality check: Microsoft's US AI-WAN is almost certainly built with a mix of self-integration, merchant optics and incumbent line-system vendors — Nokia is not confirmed in it, and you should never imply otherwise. The value of this project to you is as a reference architecture and demand signal, not a named win.
Where it becomes a Nokia deal: the ASEAN operators and hyperscalers your reports already track (Viettel/VNPT/FPT/CMC; regional cloud/DC builders in SG/Johor/Batam/HCMC) will replicate the scale-across pattern — connecting their own AI campuses with dedicated coherent DWDM. That regional DCI/long-haul build is where a Nokia line system + coherent story is directly winnable.

Research Agenda — where to dig next

The open questions this brief surfaces, to turn "read the articles" into real depth. Ordered by how much they'd sharpen the optical/DCI angle.

1
What optical layer actually underpins the AI-WAN? Microsoft names no coherent vendor, baud rate, line rate, or dark-fiber-vs-leased split. Hunt for teardown/supply-chain reporting (Data Center Frontier, TeleGeography, LightCounting, Cignal AI) and any FCC/permit filings on the routes. This is the single highest-value unknown.
2
Synchronous or asynchronous training across the WAN? Sources conflict — some say "synchronous GPU-to-GPU," Microsoft's own framing implies latency-tolerant phases only. Read the "Infinite scale" post in full + the distributed-training literature (e.g. DiLoCo / async data-parallel) to understand what 11–12 ms RTT actually permits.
3
What is MRC, and how does it relate to Ultra Ethernet / RoCE? Is the AI-WAN transport Ethernet-based? Where does it sit vs the Ultra Ethernet Consortium work? Interop and lock-in implications for anyone selling into these networks.
4
The real driver: power & grid arbitrage. Quantify why multi-site beats single-site — interconnect queue times, per-grid MW caps, time-zone load-shifting. This is the business case that makes scale-across permanent, not a phase.
5
ASEAN read-across. Which regional corridors would replicate this? Map the scale-across idea onto Johor↔SG, Batam↔SG, HCMC↔Bình Dương and any cross-border AI-campus pairing — the DCI demand your accounts would actually issue.
6
Competitive optical positioning. Who is publicly winning hyperscale long-haul coherent DCI (Ciena, Infinera-now-Nokia, Cisco/Acacia, Coherent/Lumentum on components)? Build the battlecard for "AI-WAN-class" DCI before an account asks.
