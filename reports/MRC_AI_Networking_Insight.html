What MRC actually is

MRC = Multipath Reliable Connection. It is a next-generation RDMA-over-Ethernet transport protocol, led by OpenAI with Microsoft, NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom and Intel, and released as an open spec through the Open Compute Project on 5 May 2026. In one line: it is the set of changes that make ordinary Ethernet behave well enough to run a synchronous training job across 100,000+ GPUs without stalling every time a link hiccups.

Why this matters to you (the honest version)
MRC lives at the transport layer (L3/L4) over the switch/NIC fabric — that is NVIDIA / Broadcom / Arista / AMD territory, not the optical layer Nokia sells. So MRC is not a product you quote. But it matters for two reasons an optical SE should hold: (1) it is the protocol that lets Ethernet keep beating InfiniBand for AI, which decides what optics ride the fabric (800G/1.6T Ethernet pluggables, coherent for the WAN); and (2) it runs over the scale-across AI-WAN via SRv6 — so the long-haul optical layer you do sell is literally the road MRC drives on. Read this as context, not a pitch.

The problem MRC was built to kill

In a synchronous training job, thousands of GPUs must exchange gradients in lock-step. If one flow gets congested or one link fails, the whole collective stalls and GPUs sit idle. Classic RoCE makes this worse: it pins each transfer to a single path so packets arrive in order, which causes flow collisions and hotspots. MRC attacks exactly this:

  • Congestion that idles GPUs during synchronous training.
  • Link / switch failures that used to force job restarts or slow reroutes.
  • Control-plane complexity — too many failure modes in dynamic routing.
  • Tier explosion — conventional fabrics need 3–4 switch tiers to reach 100k+ GPUs.

How MRC works — four moves

MRC is really four ideas stacked together. None is exotic on its own; the combination is what makes a 100k-GPU Ethernet fabric survivable.

1 · Multi-plane fabric

Instead of one fat 800G link per GPU, MRC splits it into eight 100G planes to eight switches — eight parallel Clos networks. Abundant paths mean you can reach 131k GPUs in just two tiers.

Same 51.2 Tb/s switch ASIC, wired as 512×100G instead of 64×800G — more paths, not more speed.

2 · Packet spray + RDMA addressing

MRC sprays one transfer across hundreds of paths. Packets can arrive out of order because each carries its final memory address (the RDMA trick) — the receiver drops each into the right place regardless of order.

This is what removes the single-path constraint that made classic RoCE collide.

3 · SRv6 source routing

Rather than BGP and dynamic route tables, the sender picks each packet's path by embedding switch IDs into the IPv6 destination address (SRv6). Routing tables are static, set once. No dynamic recalculation, one fewer class of failures.

Because eight paths exist to any endpoint, adaptive routing is unnecessary — the sender just reacts.

4 · Trimming, retransmit & healing

MRC uses packet trimming + selective retransmit + µs-scale failure detection. On packet loss it assumes the path is dead, stops using it, and reroutes — no job restart. The fabric "heals around" the break.

Congestion control is ECN-based; the result is "essentially no congestion in the core."

The strategic read

MRC's whole play is "extend RoCE, don't start over." Where Ultra Ethernet is a clean-slate protocol that needs a fresh silicon/software cycle, MRC layers onto RoCE hardware that already ships — so it can run today on NVIDIA/AMD/Broadcom NICs. That pragmatism, plus an open OCP release, is designed to make Ethernet the default AI fabric and blunt both InfiniBand and any single-vendor lock. Some coverage even frames it as a "TCP/IP moment" for AI datacenter networking.

Optics / Nokia Read

The honest SE translation: MRC is above your layer, but it moves the ground under the optics market. This tab is analyst interpretation on a factual base.

Set expectations first: MRC is a NIC + switch transport protocol (NVIDIA / Broadcom / AMD / Arista). Nokia does not sell into the MRC layer, and you should never imply an MRC "win." Its value to you is as context that shapes optical demand — nothing more, but that's not nothing.

Three ways MRC touches the optical layer

MRC factOptical consequence
MRC lets Ethernet scale to 100k+ GPUs, keeping it ahead of InfiniBandLocks in the Ethernet optics ecosystem — 800G → 1.6T pluggables (DR/FR) for scale-out, and coherent for the WAN. The fabric you feed with optics stays Ethernet.
MRC's multi-plane design trades port speed for port count (512×100G vs 64×800G)Shifts the pluggable mix — more lower-speed ports and breakout optics per switch; more transceivers per GPU, not fewer.
MRC rides the scale-across AI-WAN via SRv6 across sitesThe long-haul coherent DWDM / DCI layer you do sell is the physical road MRC drives between data centers — MRC is the tenant, your line system is the highway.

Where Nokia does / doesn't sit

Where you play: the optical transport under the fabric — DWDM line systems + coherent (1830 GX/PSS, PSE-6s) for the metro/long-haul DCI that carries MRC between sites, and the ZR/ZR+ pluggable ecosystem at the IP-optical edge. MRC growing = the AI back-end growing = more of this.
Where you don't: the NIC, the switch ASIC and the transport protocol itself — that's NVIDIA/Broadcom/AMD/Arista. Don't position Nokia as an "AI fabric" vendor at that layer.
The useful move: use MRC as credibility in a hyperscaler conversation — showing you understand why their back-end is Ethernet, why it's multi-plane, and how their cross-site training rides your optical layer. That's the SE who "gets it," which is how you earn the DCI/line-system conversation.

Research Agenda — where to dig next

Open threads to turn this into deeper knowledge.

1
Read the OpenAI/OCP paper in full. "Resilient AI Supercomputer Networking using MRC and SRv6" — the primary source for the topology math, congestion-control detail, and the exact SRv6 encoding. Everything else summarises it.
2
MRC over the WAN — how far does SRv6 stretch? Intra-site MRC is clear; the interesting question for optics is how MRC/SRv6 behaves across the ~700-mile AI-WAN (latency, reliability, whether it's the same transport or a WAN variant). Tie back to the Microsoft brief's Research Agenda #2.
3
MRC vs Ultra Ethernet — who wins the standard? Both target AI Ethernet; MRC is incremental, UEC is clean-slate. Track OCP vs UEC adoption, and whether NVIDIA/Broadcom converge them. Decides the long-run NIC/switch optics roadmap.
4
Optics content per GPU under multi-plane. Quantify how 512×100G-style wiring changes transceiver/breakout demand vs 64×800G — the real "more optics per GPU" claim. Component-market angle (Coherent, Lumentum, InnoLight).
5
ASEAN read-across. As regional operators build AI back-ends, do they adopt MRC/Ethernet or buy turnkey InfiniBand? Whichever they pick, map the optical pull — and where Nokia's DCI layer attaches.
